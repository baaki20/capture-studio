# Capture Studio 0.1.0

The first release: a screen recorder for lessons, with an editor built for teaching videos.
Linux only (GNOME on Wayland is the tested desktop). Licensed under GPL-3.0-or-later.

## Recording

- Records the screen, the camera, the microphone and the system audio together, each to its own
  file, so they stay separately editable.
- Full screen or a region. The region is picked on a still of the shared screen, with a
  magnifier and arrow-key nudging.
- Pause and resume, a countdown, restart and abort, from the controls window, the tray (with the
  AppIndicator extension) or the command line (`capture-studio --action toggle-recording`,
  `toggle-pause`, `stop-recording`, …).
- Global shortcuts through GNOME custom shortcuts that run those commands.
- Live device previews: camera with its delivered frame rate, and mic and system-audio meters.
- Streams stay in sync: each stream is calibrated against a shared session clock, and
  measured clock drift of the mic, system audio, screen and camera is corrected when the
  recording ends. System audio is captured from PipeWire, with the output latency taken into
  account.
- Unplugging a camera or mic during a recording keeps the recording going without it, and a
  notice names the device.
- Crash recovery: recordings are written as fragmented MP4 as they happen. After a crash, the
  next launch offers to recover the recording, losing at most about 2 seconds.
- Recordings land in a library of `.cstudio` project folders.

## Editor

- A multi-track timeline with three separately editable parts: the screen, the face and voice
  (linked), and the system audio.
- A picture-in-picture face overlay: corner and size presets, shape, corner radius, mirroring
  and crop. The screen can be cropped too.
- Split, cut a range, delete and ripple delete, mark in and out, snapping, undo and redo,
  frame stepping and shuttle playback (J/K/L), all with keyboard shortcuts.
- Waveforms and thumbnails on the timeline.
- A sync nudge for Face + Voice and for System audio that never alters the recorded files.
- A WebGL preview that uses the same render plan as the export. It renders ahead by its own
  measured delay to the screen, so picture and sound stay in sync while playing.
- Autosave.
- Export to MP4 (H.264 through the bundled FFmpeg) at Draft, Standard or High quality.

## Packages

- `capture-studio-0.1.0-x86_64.AppImage`: needs `fusermount3`, not `libfuse2`.
- `capture-studio_0.1.0_amd64.deb`: installs to `/opt/Capture Studio/` with a
  `/usr/bin/capture-studio` link and an AppArmor profile for Ubuntu 24.04 and later.
- Both bundle FFmpeg n9.0.2 (GPL build) with its licence and source offer.

## Verification

Checked live on GNOME Wayland (an Ubuntu laptop) with a person answering the portal:

- Sync, measured with a flashing and beeping test pattern: screen ↔ mic −3.8 ms, screen ↔
  system audio 0.1 ms, exported mix −1.7 ms, after a pause −32 ms, after crash recovery
  −4.4 ms (the target is 40 ms or less).
- A 10-minute recording is ready 3.3 s after Stop (the target is 10 s or less).
- Cancelling the share dialog, stopping from GNOME's sharing indicator, unplugging the headset
  mid-recording, the tray with and without the AppIndicator extension, and the global shortcuts
  all behave as specified.

Checked automatically (Xvfb, software rendering):

- Sync in the editor preview, measured on the picture it draws and the sound it plays:
  0.5–29 ms (it was 55–110 ms before the preview rendered ahead).
- The in-app screen picker on X11: both screens offered with thumbnails, Esc cancels, the
  chosen screen is recorded and highlighted next time. Xvfb cannot make a second monitor, so
  the test lists a stand-in second screen; everything after the list is real.

The 60-minute soak (GNOME Wayland, 1920×1080 region with camera, mic and system audio) passed:
0.00 % dropped screen and camera frames, memory 1626 → peak 1727 MB after the first 5 minutes,
0.79 % late preview frames with no audio underruns, and a Draft export of the hour that
matches the timeline to the frame. It also showed the mic drifting 18 ppm uncorrected, which
this release fixes (see Known issues).

A requirements check of this release against the Phase 1 spec: 48 of 53 requirements pass,
3 are partial, 0 fail and 2 are unverified. All 873 unit and integration tests and 11 end-to-end tests pass.

Full reports are in `docs/verification/`.

## Known issues

- GNOME asks which screen to share at every recording (the portal's restore token is not
  supported yet).
- Encoding is in software (H.264 and Opus); hardware encoders are not used yet.
- If the sound output device changes during a recording (for example, unplugging a headset
  that was playing), system audio from that point on drifts early.
- Mic drift under 20 ppm used to be left uncorrected; the soak's mic (18 ppm) ended an hour
  82 ms behind the screen. Drift is now corrected whenever it would move a track 10 ms over
  the recording, and export applies any correction of 1 ppm or more. From the soak's
  measurements, about 4 ppm (around 13 ms over an hour) remains on top of a 20–30 ms start
  offset; this has not been re-measured with a new recording.
- On systems that block unprivileged user namespaces (Ubuntu 24.04 and later), the AppImage
  starts with Chromium's sandbox turned off (`--no-sandbox`), because the sandbox cannot run
  there. The .deb installs an AppArmor profile and keeps the sandbox on.
- Not yet verified:
  - region recording on a second monitor with fractional scaling;
  - the in-app screen picker on X11 with two real monitors.
